【failure_analysis 原文保真锚点 — 主角 Weidai，禁止从公司名杜撰死因，一切以原文为准】

Weidai's collapse was primarily driven by China's systematic regulatory crackdown on P2P lending, which began in earnest in 2016 and accelerated through 2020. The Chinese government, alarmed by widespread fraud, Ponzi schemes, and social instability from platform failures, implemented increasingly strict regulations: mandatory registration, capital requirements, prohibition of credit enhancement, and ultimately a directive to exit the P2P business entirely. By 2020, regulators explicitly stated that all P2P platforms must either transform into licensed financial institutions or shut down. Weidai, like virtually all Chinese P2P lenders, could not meet the stringent licensing requirements (which required bank-level capital and governance) and faced an existential regulatory threat.

The second critical factor was a systemic liquidity and default crisis. P2P platforms operate on a maturity mismatch model—borrowing short-term from investors and lending longer-term to borrowers. When investor confidence eroded (due to high-profile platform failures like Ezubao's $7.6B Ponzi scheme), platforms faced bank-run dynamics. Weidai experienced withdrawal surges while simultaneously seeing borrower default rates spike as economic conditions deteriorated and regulatory uncertainty made collections harder. The platform likely had to use new investor funds to pay existing investors—a death spiral. Unlike banks, P2P platforms lacked deposit insurance, central bank liquidity access, or government backstops, making them structurally fragile during credit cycles.

The third factor was adverse selection and unit economics deterioration. As regulations tightened and competition intensified, Weidai faced a classic lemons problem: the best borrowers migrated to banks and licensed fintech (Ant Financial, WeBank) offering lower rates and better user experience, leaving P2P platforms with riskier borrowers. ... By 2023, with the regulatory window closed, investor base evaporated, and loan book deteriorated, Weidai had no viable path forward and ceased operations, joining thousands of failed Chinese P2P platforms in one of fintech's most spectacular industry-wide collapses.
